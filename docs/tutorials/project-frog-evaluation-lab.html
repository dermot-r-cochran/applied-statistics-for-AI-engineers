Progressive tutorial

Project Frog evaluation lab

A fictional triage model estimates whether a frog sighting needs expert review. Move step by step and decide what evidence is strong enough to change the workflow.

Progress:
Step 1

Define the decision first

The model does not replace experts. It decides whether to route a case to manual review within the fictional amphibian monitoring workflow.

If you cannot name the operational decision, accuracy alone will not tell you whether the system is safe or useful.

Step 2

Sample the environment you care about

Project Frog has wetland, forest, and urban habitats. A test set drawn only from wetlands would overstate reliability if the production mix shifts later.

  • Capture the intended deployment mix.
  • Check whether rare but high-impact cases are represented.
  • Document exclusions and missing labels.
Step 3

Interrogate confidence, not just labels

A model that assigns 0.95 to many wrong cases is harder to govern than a model that is uncertain in the right places.

Use calibration checks to ask whether predicted confidence matches observed frequency.

Step 4

Compare runs with the same yardstick

When you compare model A and model B, keep the test set, label policy, and routing threshold fixed. Otherwise the comparison is confounded.

Step 5

Turn evidence into action

Evaluation ends with a decision: ship, hold, or collect more evidence. Good practice makes the rationale explicit and reversible.

Decision:

Evidence checklist

Question Why it matters
Is the evaluation sample representative? Without this, measured quality may not transfer to deployment.
Are confidence scores calibrated? Thresholds and downstream automation depend on reliable probabilities.
Is the comparison apples-to-apples? Comparing different datasets or label policies can create fake wins.
